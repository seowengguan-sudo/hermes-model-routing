## Concept — designing the Human-in-the-Loop checkpoint (so the guardrail doesn't become the new bottleneck)
- **The dial tells you WHEN to escalate, not HOW to survive the escalations.** `flag_block_risk` → `should_block=Y` → human decides. At 40 C&I projects, even "block cases only" can be 8/week. A naive design pages the PM for every one, same-day, 10 min each → the guardrail becomes the constraint you just automated around. The safety fence re-creates the bottleneck.
- **Triage by consequence-timeline, not by the flag.** A lapsed TNB approval with install imminent = HIGH (slip = 2-wk delay + penalty → page now). A "pending" permit with 3 weeks of runway = LOW → batch into a Friday review. Route by *how fast the consequence lands*, not by the binary Y/N.
- **Batch + SLA beats interrupt.** HIGH pages immediately (2×10 min). LOW collects into one 30-min weekly session with a signed SLA (review within 48h). That's what turns 8 min/project/day into 2 — and it's a *design* choice, not luck. Async escalation wins at scale every time.
- **Make the human's job smaller than the automation's.** The agent pre-fills each escalation with the ONE fact that mattered (permit lapsed 6 days ago; project X slips 3 weeks) + its own recommendation. Human = 30-sec confirm/override, not a re-investigation. This is where the cost-axis saving actually materializes: $/run drops only if the checkpoint is cheap to operate.
- **The checkpoint is also your data engine.** Every human decision — especially an override — writes back to `review_queue` → feeds `drift_monitor`'s `golden_v2` re-audit. Escalation isn't just a gate; it's how the golden set stays alive (reuses the 2026-08-14 drift lesson).
- **Three lenses, one decision:** the cost-axis saving (2026-08-16 AM) is ONLY real if the checkpoint is triaged + batched + bounded. Safety (guardrail) + throughput (checkpoint design) + economics (cost axis) are three views of the same design call. Miss the checkpoint design and the 2-min promise is fiction.

## Test (scenario — price the checkpoint)

- **(a)** Naive design: every block pages the PM same-day, 10 min each. Weekly PM cost, and does it re-create the bottleneck?
- **(b)** Triaged design: HIGH pages immediately (10 min each), LOW batched into one 30-min Friday session. Weekly PM cost now, and does it fit inside the dial8 budget of 2 min/proj/day (80 min/day across 40 projects)?
- **(c)** Agent pre-fills each escalation so human decision = 30-sec confirm. By how much does that cut the LOW batch time?
- **(d)** Where does each human decision get written so the checkpoint also feeds `golden_v2`?

*(Expected: (a) 8×10=80 min/wk × $40/hr = ~$53/wk; synchronous paging re-creates the PM bottleneck (the very thing automation removed). (b) HIGH 2×10=20 + LOW 30 = 50 min/wk ≈ 10 min/day, far inside the 80 min/day budget, and async (no interrupts) → fits. (c) LOW batch 6×0.5=3 min + ~2 min setup ≈ 5 min vs 30 → ~83% cut. (d) every decision → `review_queue` → drift_monitor quarantine/re-label → promote `golden_v2`.)*
