## Concept — the autonomy dial is a cost dial too
- **The dial is a safety control you already know — but every position also has a $/decision cost.** Shadow (dial 0): you pay the LLM to read state + flag + score on every project, yet a human still does the work → cost with zero labor saved. Enforce (dial 10): same LLM cost, but you also save the human's minutes per decision. The dial moves both risk AND spend at once.
- **In agentic loops, cost = tool calls × tokens.** `auto_schedule_crew` at canary "act on low-risk only" fires `get_permit_status` + `flag_block_risk` + `propose_schedule` on every project; enforce ADDS `commit_schedule` + the recall-gate re-score. Each extra call = more tokens = more $. The loop's *shape*, not just the model, sets the bill.
- **The break-even the Director actually signs is $/compliance-miss-avoided.** A missed block can cost a TNB penalty + 2-week delay (real $); the agent might cost $0.02/run. The math favors automation — but you must SHOW the number, not assert it. That quantified business case IS your cost-optimization value-add.
- **Trap given your "free-tier only" habit:** free local models hide this. In production you pay per token; design the loop to minimize calls (cache permit status, batch the nightly `score_eval.py`, only commit on the low-risk class) so $/run stays flat as autonomy rises. The cheapest guardrail is the call you didn't have to make.
- **Connection you may have missed:** the recall gate (R5/R7) isn't only safety — it's also the cost ceiling. A breach → auto-rollback to dial 0 → you pay full LLM cost with zero autonomy benefit (worst of both). Keeping recall high is *also* keeping the system cost-efficient. Safety and economics point the same way.
