**Run 5 lesson (for your review):**

Last lesson gave you *evals* — a fixed test set + a score. But the score you pick decides whether you ship something **safe** or something that only **looks** safe. Plain accuracy hides the exact failure that matters most in a guardrail.

- **Confusion matrix = the 2×2 that names every mistake:** TP (right to flag) / FP (false alarm) / FN (missed it) / TN (right to pass). Accuracy = (TP+TN)/all — but it blends the two mistakes into one number.
- **Precision = TP/(TP+FP)** — of the times you said "block," how many really were. Low precision = you cry wolf (annoying, usually harmless).
- **Recall = TP/(TP+FN)** — of the real risks, how many you caught. Low recall = you let danger through (the dangerous one).
- **Why accuracy lies:** if only 3 of 20 projects truly needed a block, a tool that *never blocks* scores 85% accuracy and catches ZERO real risks (recall = 0%). For a safety guardrail, that 85% books a crew before the permit clears.
- **PENSOLAR:** the `auto_schedule_crew` guardrail. The positive class = permit-not-cleared, must-block (rare, ~3/20). **Gate on recall (~100%)**; track precision for false-alarm cost. Show accuracy to the Director *last*, not as the ship gate.
- **POC takeaway:** when you build the 20-case set, report the 2×2 for every guardrail tool and write the **recall** cutoff into the spec for any irreversible action.
